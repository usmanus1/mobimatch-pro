How to Fix Mobile Fake Charging, Slow Charge & Primary Dead Shorts

Updated: November 2026 • By MobiMatch Pro Hardware Bench Team • 5 min read

Fake charging—where the phone shows the lightning charging icon on screen but the battery percentage never increases (or drops)—is one of the most common motherboard faults in mobile repair workshops. Following a structured multimeter sequence prevents unnecessary PMIC or CPU heating.

1. Check USB Ammeter Draw & D+/D- Cold GR

  • 0.00A Draw: Inspect the USB-C/Micro-USB jack, CC sub-board flex, and the 28V OVP IC (e.g., WS3210, KTS1682).
  • 0.10A – 0.45A Draw (Fake Charging): Disconnect battery and charger, set multimeter to Diode Mode (Red probe on Ground), and measure USB D+ and D- lines. Both must show symmetrical readings (450mV–750mV).

2. The 25V Bootstrap Capacitor (BTST) Fix

Roughly 70% of fake charging faults on BQ25890H, SY6970, PMI632, MT6360PP, and S2MU106X01 boards are caused by a degraded 0402 22nF–47nF 25V Bootstrap Capacitor connected between the BTST/BOOT pin and the VPH_PWR switching buck coil. Always replace it with a ≥25V rated ceramic capacitor—a 6.3V capacitor will fail immediately.

3. Safe Voltage Injection for Dead Shorts

If VBAT or VPH_PWR reads 0.000V in Diode Mode, lift the VPH_PWR buck coil first to see which side is shorted to ground:

  • Primary Rail Short (VBAT / VPH_PWR): Coat the board in white rosin smoke and inject 3.8V–4.0V (3A limit) in short 0.5-second pulses to spot the shorted ceramic capacitor.
  • Secondary Rail Short (CPU / RAM / 1.8V LDO): Never inject 4.0V into secondary buck lines! Inject strictly 0.8V–1.0V for CPU/RAM rails or 1.8V for VIO18 rails.
